BOILERPLATE (100 words)
House of Kani is a fourth-generation Kashmiri family house in Hazratbal, Srinagar. It sells 100% pure Pashmina — handwoven Kani, hand-embroidered Sozni and fine solids — by private enquiry, with worldwide delivery and appointments at its looms. The family works with artisans it has known for years, dyes a 279-shade solid range, and explains the fibre through its Journal: the Changthangi goat, the 16-micron Kashmir GI specification, the talim, Sozni stitch vocabulary, care, repair and the legal position on shahtoosh. Every piece is shown by photograph and measurement before it is sent.

KEY FACTS
- Fourth-generation family house, Hazratbal, Srinagar, Jammu and Kashmir.
- 100% pure Pashmina: Kani, Sozni and solids.
- 279 solid shades.
- Private-enquiry model; worldwide delivery; appointments in Srinagar or by video.
- The Journal publishes long-form guides on the fibre, GI, craft, care and buying.
- The house does not sell shahtoosh and explains why the trade is prohibited.

STORY ANGLES
1. What the Kashmir Pashmina GI actually certifies — and what it does not.
2. The talim: a woven pattern written as a spoken code.

4. The 279-shade dye house and how colour is chosen for a shawl.
5. Moth damage, repair and the economics of keeping a shawl for decades.
6. Shahtoosh: why the finest Kashmir cloth is illegal to trade.
